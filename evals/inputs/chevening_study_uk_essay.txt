Over the last five years I have worked as a nurse in the paediatric ward of Assiut University Hospital, where I see around 40 children a day. In 2021 I noticed that nearly half the mothers we discharged came back within a month because they did not understand the medication plan. I designed a one-page picture-based discharge sheet with two colleagues, and we trained 25 nurses to use it. Within six months, our readmissions for the same complaint fell from 46 to 29 per month.

Despite this, I have found myself missing the research skills to prove what works and to scale it beyond one ward. My nursing degree taught me to care for patients, not to design and evaluate health programmes.

That is why I chose three UK courses: MSc Public Health at the University of Sheffield, because its core modules in epidemiology and health promotion would give me the methods I lack; MSc Global Health at the University of Manchester, for its focus on low- and middle-income settings; and MSc Public Health at the University of Glasgow, for its strong links with the NHS.

Studying in the UK will also let me learn how the NHS runs patient education at scale, and I will share what we built in Assiut with my classmates.
