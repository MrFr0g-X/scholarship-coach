# Writing moves, with neutral examples

These are the moves strong statements use again and again. The examples are invented and generic on purpose. Show the *move* to the user, never copy an example into their essay, and build their version only from their own facts.

## 1. The moves

| Move | Pattern | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Rapid-fire hook, then pivot** | Two to four recent, specific recognitions with numbers, then "All of that happened in one year, but it took a six-year journey to get there." | Signals scale at once, then earns the story. |
| **"You might now wonder…"** | A list of results, then "You might now wonder why I was chosen for these. The answer is [the topic]." | Turns the evidence into a question that the topic answers. |
| **"Most importantly…"** | The last beat is always about others. | Ends beyond yourself; meets the selflessness test. |
| **"Despite all that, I lack…"** | Achievements first, then an honest gap. | Justifies the degree without sounding weak. |
| **Pay it forward** | Someone opened a door for me, I used it, and now I open doors for others. | Proves networking through outcomes. |
| **Concrete future scenario** | One vivid picture of the long-term goal working, with who, where, how many and how much. | Makes a dream tangible and SMART. |
| **Numbers, dates, names** | Counts, years, durations, named roles. | Verifiable, so credible. |
| **Belief line** | One sentence the applicant truly believes, stated plainly. | Memorable, and links identity to mission. |
| **Identity as signal** | First in the family at university, public schools, a working-class background, stated once and proudly. | Makes the achievements bigger, in proportion. |
| **Researched fit** | A named module, lab, ranking or focus that matches a need from the story. | Proves research; tailored. |
| **Recycling** | The same core story reused across applications, with numbers updated and the fit section rewritten. | Efficient and consistent. |

## 2. Rapid-fire hook, then the pivot
> Last spring, a national education forum invited me to speak to 1,200 teachers. In the same month, my training program was adopted by three more schools, and a regional newspaper covered our results. All of that happened in one year, but it took a six-year journey to get there.
>
> In my second year of university, I noticed that most students in my faculty had never written a CV…

**Moves:** high-signal opening, pivot line, then a STAR story with numbers.

## 3. "You might now wonder… The answer is…"
**Prompt:** "How did your project change you personally?"
> In two years I was selected for a regional youth fellowship, invited to train volunteers in four cities, and asked to join an advisory board. You might now wonder why someone with a pharmacy degree was chosen for all three. The answer is the small reading club I started in 2021.

## 4. The gap: "Despite all that, I lack…"
> I have trained more than 300 volunteers, written six training guides and run workshops in five cities. Despite all that, I have no formal training in how to evaluate whether a program works. I can count who attended, but I cannot yet prove what changed. That is the gap this degree fills.

## 5. Pay it forward (networking)
> In 2018 I joined a volunteer program knowing nothing about running a project. The coordinator, a woman who had built three community groups herself, gave me her contacts and her time. When she moved abroad, she kept advising me online. Five years later, my own group became a partner of that same program, and I now mentor 40 young volunteers the way she mentored me.

**Moves:** a named relationship (by role), what was received, what was done with it, what is now given back.

## 6. A concrete future scenario (career plan)
> My long-term goal is one platform where a small youth group can find everything it needs for a project. Picture a group of students in a coastal town planning a beach clean-up. They need 100 volunteers, a small budget and a few local voices to spread the word. The platform gives them all three in one place.

**Moves:** the dream, then one vivid scenario with numbers.

## 7. Reframing an ordinary experience
**The facts:** for six summers, the applicant helped a local charity collect donated clothes, sort them, and sell the unusable ones to a recycler. The money paid for school supplies.

**Told well:** waste reduction (clothes kept out of landfill), a small income stream for a local recycler, and school supplies for 150 children each year. It's the same work that every other volunteer did, told with its full ripple of impact.

**Lesson:** the same facts, told with layers of impact. See `modes/reframe.md`.

## 8. Recycling across applications
Keep the core stories, the belief line and the identity. Update numbers that have grown. Rewrite "why this program", the career link and anything that answers a question the new program doesn't ask. See Adapt mode in `SKILL.md`.
