# Review Rubric (10 criteria, each scored 1-5)

Score what is on the page, not what the applicant could have written. Be calibrated:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **5** | Would stand out in a pile of 100 |
| **4** | Strong; one fix away from standing out |
| **3** | Competent but forgettable |
| **2** | Clear problems |
| **1** | Missing or harmful |

Most first drafts land between 22 and 32 out of 50. Don't hand out 4s and 5s to be nice. The applicant needs to know where they really stand.

---

### 1. Hook (first line or two)
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | A specific moment or a striking concrete fact that pulls the reader straight into the story, and it connects to the next paragraph. |
| 3 | Clear but generic ("Since childhood I have been passionate about…"). |
| 1 | Throat-clearing ("My name is…", "I am writing to apply…", "To the X scholarship"), a dictionary definition, or a cliché quote. |

### 2. Answers the question asked
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Every part of the prompt is answered directly, and the essay's spine *is* the answer. |
| 3 | The question is answered, but drifts into CV recital. |
| 1 | Off-topic, or a recycled essay that doesn't fit this prompt. |

### 3. Why you: distinctiveness and signaling
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | After reading, you can say in one sentence why *this* person and not the other 99. There are clear signals (selective programs, recognition, scale). |
| 3 | Good qualities are claimed but not proven, or it could be anyone in the field. |
| 1 | Humble to the point of invisibility, or claims with no support. |

### 4. Purpose beyond yourself
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Driven by a cause bigger than the writer (community, country, region, the SDGs), shown through action already taken. It ends on others. |
| 3 | The cause is mentioned but feels bolted on. |
| 1 | Purely self-serving: career, money, travel, "new experiences". |

### 5. Evidence: numbers, dates, names
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Claims are verifiable through dates, counts, organisations, places and people. A reader could fact-check them. |
| 3 | Some numbers, but key claims stay vague ("many", "a lot of", "various"). |
| 1 | No concrete evidence at all. |

### 6. Storytelling: STAR and "how you tell it"
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | The main story has a clear Situation, Task, Action and Result. The framing shows layers of impact. Active verbs. |
| 3 | The story is there but missing the Action detail or the Result, or it's told flatly. |
| 1 | A list of roles with no story. |

### 7. Challenges and character (V-SPICE)
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Honest vulnerability or challenge, used to show perseverance and initiative, with a positive tone ("despite… I…"). Covers several V-SPICE elements. |
| 3 | The challenge is mentioned but not used, or the character is only claimed. |
| 1 | Complaining or blaming, or no sense of the person. |

### 8. Future plans and fit
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | SMART short-term and long-term plans, a clear five-year picture, and specifics about why *this* program, university or country (named modules, professors, rankings, partners). It says how the scholarship helps afterwards. |
| 3 | Plans are vague, or the fit is generic ("world-class education", "prestigious"). |
| 1 | No plan, or no reason given for this program. |

### 9. Tone and voice
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Confident without arrogance. Positive. Personal but not too personal. Sounds like one real person talking, and survives the read-aloud test. |
| 3 | Stiff, template-like, or over-modest in places. |
| 1 | Negative, preachy, jokey, or written like a third-person bio. |

### 10. Mechanics and compliance
| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Within the limit, well-paragraphed, clean grammar, balanced use of "I", and none of the 29 feedback mistakes. |
| 3 | A few errors or don'ts. |
| 1 | Over the limit, or full of errors and banned openers or closers. |

## Question-specific add-ons (check alongside the 10)

**Chevening: Leadership**
- Does it show *influence*, meaning people moved and change made, not just titles held?
- Is there a journey (a pivot like "it took a six-year journey")?
- Are there numbers for scale?

**Chevening: Networking**
- Are there real relationships with named people, both given and received?
- Is there a *pay-it-forward* pattern?
- Is there a concrete plan to network in the UK, and a reason UK contacts matter to the mission?

**Chevening: Study in the UK**
- Does it name a gap that the degree fills ("despite all I did, I lack…")?
- Does it focus on the **first-choice course** (current official guidance), connecting it to background, career and impact, while showing all three were researched? (Older guides said to cover all three equally; that is outdated.)
- Is there added value they'll share with UK peers or NGOs?

**Chevening: Career plan**
- Are there **short, mid and long-term** goals (current official guidance), each clear and measurable?
- Is the long-term "life dream" made tangible by a specific scenario with numbers?
- Does it show willingness to **collaborate with the UK** on shared challenges?
- Does it link to the degree and to the beyond-self purpose?

**University PS / SOP**
- Is there academic preparation (courses, research, skills)?
- Is there why this program, with named modules or faculty?
- Are there career goals the degree enables?

**Undergrad**
- Is the motivation for the field shown through experience?
- Are there school and extracurricular achievements, with what was learned from each?
- Is the fit with the university shown?

**Exchange program / short answer**
- Is the specific question answered within its tiny limit?
- Is there added value to the group?
- Is it clear how the experience will be used back home?

**Job or internship motivation letter**
- Why this organisation (researched)?
- Which 2-3 proofs match the role's needs?
- What will they contribute in the first months?
